\section{Tuning on Both Sides}\label{app:tuned}

The baselines of Section~5 of the paper run at one fixed setting each. This comparison was declared before the chunk-selection score S2 was adopted; the readings for \method with S2 apply its family and reading rules post hoc. Here RM3 and Bo1 are tuned per collection on the evaluation queries themselves (oracle), which favours them, and by two-fold cross-validation; \method is tuned the same way over $K$ and $w$. For RM3, tuning varies the number of feedback documents, the number of expansion terms and the interpolation weight $\lambda$. The grids differ in size: 12 settings for \method, 18 for RM3 and 6 for Bo1, and the oracles often chose settings at the edge of their grid ($K = 10$ and $w = 0.30$ for \method, $\lambda = 0.7$ for RM3). The query sets are capped at 500 queries per collection.
Table~\ref{tab:B-tuned-prf} gives the comparison with tuned PRF summarised in Section~5.1 of the paper. When both sides are tuned on the test queries (oracle against oracle), \method is better or non-inferior in 14 of 16 contrasts and never worse (Table~\ref{tab:B-both-tuned}); with S2 alone, in 7 of 8. In each of the four settings, cross-validated tuning adds less on average to \method with S2 than to RM3 and to Bo1 (Table~\ref{tab:B-headroom}), although against Bo1 the ranges over the 30 splits overlap in three of the four settings (both over DPH and over BM25 over 1M): averaged over 30 random splits, it adds between $-0.0001$ and $+0.0036$ nDCG@10 to the fixed setting of \method with S2, against $+0.0024$ to $+0.0158$ for RM3 and Bo1. Tuned PRF still lowers several collections (Table~\ref{tab:B-tuned-risk}). Per collection, \method (S2) at its fixed setting is ahead of the best-of-both oracle by more than 0.005 on 10 collections, behind it on 9 and within $\pm 0.005$ on 6 over BM25, and 9, 10 and 6 over DPH.

\section{Cost of the Research Implementation}\label{app:cost}

Table~\ref{tab:C-build} gives the offline build cost per collection and Table~\ref{tab:C-latency} the query latency on the experiment machine. Our latency is that of a research implementation (Python, through PyTerrier) set against Terrier's native PRF implementations. Most of the overhead of \method over BM25 is implementation cost, in the per-call PyTerrier overhead of the lexical profile channel of Gate~1 and in the Python-side query encoding, rather than cost inherent to the method: the retrieval with the five added terms itself costs about 5 to 17~ms more than BM25 when the query is encoded on the CPU. Native implementations of Gate~1 and Gate~2 are expected to narrow the gap to PRF. LLM-Q2E takes 604 to 757~ms per query; the timed generations are fresh and differ from the cached generations of the effectiveness runs on 44\% of the timed queries. On the five large collections the build figures are lower bounds, since the co-occurrence lists were computed only for the anchors of the evaluated queries.
